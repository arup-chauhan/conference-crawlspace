# Explain conference material

Use this workflow to explain talks or create illustrated reports and decks.

## Build source briefs before drafting

For each selected talk, record:

- Identity: exact title, speaker, edition, and source origin.
- Inspected evidence: slide pages, recording timestamps, transcript, or abstract only.
- Problem: the starting architecture, difficulty, and why it mattered.
- Mechanism: what changed, who acts, what moves, and how the steps connect.
- Outcome: reported result, baseline, and conditions, or no available result.
- Limits: prerequisites, correctness boundaries, unfinished work, and uncertainty.
- Connection: the user's interest and links to other sources.

Read the source body. Titles alone cannot support a source brief.

Inspect diagrams and chart labels visually when they carry the explanation. Sparse panel slides do not establish what panelists said.

Distinguish proposed topics, actual discussion, and adopted changes in community material. Future agenda entries are not completed meetings.

Use official technical documentation to fill explanatory gaps when useful. Label it as supplemental material, not verified talk content.

Look for alternate public recordings or speaker material before declaring a gap. Preserve the gap if no substantive source is available.

## Use simple models accurately

Use a concrete example or analogy to explain unfamiliar concepts. Map it back to the real components.

State where the model simplifies behavior and what it cannot establish. Mark invented values as illustrative.

A teaching unit usually needs a problem, an illustrated mechanism, and an implication or limit. Show the mechanism in a few connected steps.

Choose visuals that explain relationships, movement, sequences, or tradeoffs. Decorative infrastructure art alone does not explain an architecture.

Use animation to reveal how the mechanism works. Keep the complete explanation readable.

Explain each source's contribution before combining ideas across talks. A combined architecture is your interpretation unless a source presents that whole system.

Label speaker performance claims as reported. Preserve denominators and workload conditions. Draft figures are not established findings.

## Check scope and completion

Map the agreed topics to relevant talks and source briefs. Broad requests need coverage across that inventory.

Put items with limited evidence in an explicit coverage section. Do not give them the same weight as inspected decks.

Before delivery, check:

- Does the output explain what the covered speakers actually did?
- Can a reader describe each main mechanism without opening the source?
- Do the illustrations teach how it works?
- Are source findings, analogies, inferences, and proposed experiments clearly distinct?
- Do the stated gaps match the unavailable material?

For learning requests, teach first. Recommendations and experiments may follow, but must not displace the explanations.
